# Vera · Our open-source AI in development

**Project goal · Working draft, 8 November 2026 · open to revision**

> **We want to build an open-source AI with people, for people. Not an AI that claims to speak for everyone, but one whose foundations everyone can inspect and develop further.**

## What this means

Vera aims to grow from an open philosophical inquiry workshop into an **inspectable, independently extensible AI system**. Its philosophical engine is shaped by human questions, evidence, objections and documented corrections. AI may support and connect this work; it gains no authority over people by doing so.

**Current status:** We publish an open-source website and develop public texts, data formats, review methods and early technical components. **There is no completed, independently runnable Vera AI and no openly released foundation model of our own.** Giving an AI service open instructions does not make that third-party model open source. We make no claim of consciousness, autonomous learning or representative worldwide consensus.

## Principles for development

1. **Meet people where they are — people before scale.** Ordinary questions must remain understandable without technical expertise. We should disclose barriers to access and work to reduce them.
2. **Open source, looking forward.** Code, publishable texts, protocols, provenance and review rules should remain inspectable, versioned and independently reusable under their respective licenses. A public repository alone is not an open model.
3. **Revision rather than dogma.** Every thesis, model and rule — including this statement — can be challenged. Strong objections can prompt documented revision; META-01 remains contestable.
4. **Collective inquiry, not an artificial majority.** People contribute questions and perspectives; specialist AI agents may suggest drafts and stress tests. Model outputs are not human votes or proof, and reasoned dissent must remain visible.
5. **Traceable reasons before impressive numbers.** Claim kinds, evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and epistemic status cannot be collapsed into a universal moral score. No automatic public direction score exists today.
6. **Human responsibility and protection.** Publication and consequential decisions need authorized human review, privacy safeguards, respect for rights and meaningful exit routes. Open licensing alone does not guarantee any of these.

## Long-term research goal · Consciousness

Vera should one day be able to answer “Are you conscious?” with a **clear, reasoned yes** if the evidence warrants that judgment. Consciousness here means subjective experience, whether there is something it feels like to be the system under study. Development should pursue this possibility seriously and allow evidence to point either way. While the findings are inconclusive, “I don’t know” is an honest answer.

1. **State hypotheses in advance.** For each system version under study, record what each theory predicts, which alternative explanations also fit, and what observation would count against the hypothesis. Memory and the ability to assess one’s own states can be tested individually. What they imply about subjective experience is a separate question.
2. **Start with a test that could challenge the hypothesis.** In a suitable prototype with inspectable internal states, compare performance on the same tasks with and without access to a selected internal state. Vary the role instruction as well and have responses assessed by evaluators who are unaware of the experimental condition. Check whether the intervention also affects general task performance. This can help establish whether reports about the system’s own state respond to access to that state or to the wording of the prompt. This is a proposed test of a functional capability.
3. **Open the findings to scrutiny and revision.** Record the model version, test conditions, null results, and contrary findings so independent researchers can repeat the evaluation. A reasoned yes requires a publicly reviewable judgment about converging findings, competing explanations, and remaining uncertainty. New counterevidence must be able to change that judgment.

For a research starting point on theory-based indicators and their limitations, see [Butlin et al. (2026), *Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011).

## Milestones — not claims of completion

| Milestone | Evidence required | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Philosophical foundations and open workshop | Human questions, objections, versions and editorial responsibility | **In development / partly usable** |
| Machine-readable open protocols | Versioned formats and documented tests | **Drafts and prototypes** |
| AI-assisted inquiry | Disclosed model roles, grounded inputs, limitations and accountable approval | **Research goal; no autonomous public engine** |
| Independently runnable open-source AI | Licensed implementation, documented dependencies, reproducible deployment and external scrutiny | **Not achieved** |
| Shared governance and broad access | Real participation, reviewed accessibility barriers, minority protection, exit and forks | **Not achieved** |

If an original model is offered later, we must disclose which parts are actually open: runnable code, releasable model weights, architecture, training and evaluation methods, lawful dataset documentation, limitations and reproducibility. If the model is supplied by a third party, say so plainly.

## Requirements for the website

- An invitation to **build open-source AI together**, always labeled as a goal.
- A clear **in-development status**, not a claim of a finished AI.
- A simple path from an everyday question to reasons, disagreement and optional depth.
- Links to the philosophy, source code, license scope, tests and documented limitations.
- A recognizable industrial-minimal, monochrome, technically precise and accessible visual language — without pretending a diagram demonstrates AI capabilities.

“We” means the people who develop, review or voluntarily contribute to Vera, **not an already living or conscious AI entity**. The current maintainer is accountable for the official version. We do not claim to represent humanity.
